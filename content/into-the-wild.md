# Into the wild

In January 2014, Johan Bergenäs took me to Ngulia Rhino Sanctuary in Kenya.

I had been interested in nature since I was a boy. But this was different.

There were rhinos worth protecting, poachers trying to kill them, and rangers expected to protect an enormous area with very limited resources.

As an engineer, it was difficult not to start thinking.

The first useful thing we built wasn't a sensor.

> **It was an app.**

Rangers needed better information before they needed better gadgets.

The gadgets came later.

**Phones → tracking tags → cameras → drones → radar → microphones → geophones → AI → BLE scanners in acacia trees**

What started as a security project gradually became something else: a place where engineers, biologists, students, rangers and conservationists could work on the same problems from completely different directions.

Students started coming with us to Africa. Their MSc projects stopped being PDFs and became things they had to install, explain and make work.

Power fails. Networks disappear. Animals don't read specifications. Users have opinions.

> **Excellent research environment.**

More than ten years later, I'm still climbing trees.
