# Into the wild

In January 2014, Johan Bergenäs took me to Ngulia Rhino Sanctuary in Kenya.

I had been interested in nature since I was a boy. But this was different.

There were endangered rhinos worth protecting, poachers trying to kill them, and rangers expected to patrol an enormous protected area with limited resources and difficult conditions.

As an engineer, it was difficult not to start thinking.

The first useful thing we built wasn't a sensor.

> **It was an app.**

**WildProtect** digitised patrol and wildlife observations so rangers and commanders could build shared situational awareness. Rangers needed better information before they needed better gadgets.

![Fredrik Gustafsson field testing a hovering drone for sensing and navigation.](imagedronepilot.png "The gadgets came later.")

The gadgets came later.

**Phones → tracking tags → cameras → drones → radar → microphones → geophones → AI → BLE scanners in acacia trees**

**Wildlife Security** grew into a research programme combining positioning, camera traps, tracking tags, radar, acoustic and seismic sensing, communications, AI and decision support.

What started as a security project gradually became a place where engineers, biologists, students, rangers and conservationists could work on the same problems from completely different directions.

Students started coming with us to Africa. Their MSc projects stopped being PDFs and became systems they had to install, explain, debug and leave working.

![An overhead collection of trail cameras, antennas, lights, cables, chargers and field electronics arranged on a wooden table.](imagegadgets.png "Some assembly required.")

Power fails. Networks disappear. Animals don't read specifications. Users have opinions.

Heat, dust, scarce power, unreliable connectivity, limited ground truth, false alarms and maintenance are part of the system—not inconveniences outside it.

> **Excellent research environment.**

The direction is now broader: animal behaviour, livestock, conservation, coexistence and environmental monitoring. Qulinda and Smart Savannahs connect that work to practical systems and long-term conservation efforts without changing the central question: what is happening, where, and does someone need to know?

![Fredrik Gustafsson standing beside a solar-powered super-sensor mast with rhinos and Mount Kenya behind him at Ol Pejeta, Kenya.](imageolpejetatower.jpg "Ol Pejeta, Kenya. || With the super-sensor mast.")

More than ten years later, I'm still climbing trees.

![Fredrik Gustafsson with two rhinos behind him at Ol Pejeta in Kenya; the Smart Savannahs logo remains visible on his shirt.](imageselfierhino.png "Ol Pejeta, Kenya.")
